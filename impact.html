Impact

What changes when a book is scanned once.

Most of India’s books sit on one shelf in one library, and only the people who can reach that shelf can read them. Once SOK scans a book and makes it searchable, it can reach all of these people.

First in India: in 2018, SOK became the first digital archiving project in India to bring OCR in Indian languages to Internet Archive collections, starting with the Public Library of India and the Gazettes of India, and pushed the text back to the Archive.

01A student or professor who cannot read print

NLSIU began this project because the university wanted its visually challenged students to have access. SOK digitised all 33,149 books and, with IDIA, built a legal database. Visually impaired professors use it too. Searchable text can be read aloud or turned into Braille.

02A researcher or student far from the archive

Instead of travelling to the one library that holds a rare volume, researchers search the full text of every page. Students at Kannada University, Hampi depend heavily on the digitised PhD and MPhil theses, and Karnataka Samskrit University uses the resources for research on Sanskrit literature.

03A teacher with a class to teach

Universities and institutions use the scanned books to build course packs. An old book or a translated work can be found, read online and shared as one link. Azim Premji University’s collection of 4,944 items is open to read.

04A library or institution

A fragile collection is kept safe and stays in reach. Partners get their scans back, and offline libraries that work without internet: 20,000 books for Science Gallery Bengaluru and 12,000+ for the RBANM Trust.

05Anyone with a phone

A Gandhi book, a Kannada novel or an old magazine, free to read online. The Servants Of Knowledge collection has 89,000+ items and more than 1.8 crore views and downloads.

06The people who scan

Every centre trains and employs local technicians, so the work builds skills and jobs along with the archive.
