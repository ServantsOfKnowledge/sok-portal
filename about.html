About

Gyan Satyagraha: opening knowledge to everyone.

Servants Of Knowledge began as two people scanning books in their spare time. Today it is a registered non-profit running open-hardware digitisation centres across India.

CM

Carl Malamud

Co-founder & Honorary Secretary

US-based technologist and open-data advocate; founder of Public.Resource.Org. Called "The Internet's Own Instigator" by Wired. Three decades spent making government records, laws and standards public, from the SEC's EDGAR database to India's Bureau of Indian Standards.

Omshivaprakash H.L.

Co-founder & CEO

Bengaluru-based digital archivist and language technologist, with over 20 years in Kannada technology. Founded Sanchaya (2010) and co-founded Sanchi Foundation (2014); Wikipedia editor since 2007. Leads scanner engineering, operations and centre mobilisation across India. Profile ↗

SS

Dr. Sushant Sinha

Founding Director & CTO

Computer scientist and founder of Indian Kanoon, India's leading free legal research platform. Leads OCR, search infrastructure and Indian-language processing for SOK's collections.

Dean, School of Legal and Socio-Political Studies, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar University, Delhi. Guides SOK's approach to Indian copyright law so every collection is digitised and shared within it.

Who it helps

What changes when a book is scanned once

Most of India’s books sit on one shelf in one library, and only the people who can reach that shelf can read them. Once SOK scans a book and makes it searchable, it can reach all of these people.

01A student who cannot read print

A searchable text can be read aloud or turned into Braille. At NLSIU, SOK digitised the whole library of 33,149 books and, with IDIA, built a legal database for visually-challenged students.

02A researcher far from the archive

Instead of travelling to the one library that holds a rare volume, a researcher searches the full text of every page and cites the exact page. Research Desk does this for 88,000+ books, in Kannada, Hindi, Tamil and other languages.

03A teacher with a class to teach

An old book or a translated work can be found, read online and shared as one link. Azim Premji University’s collection of 4,944 items is open to read.

04A library or institution

A fragile collection is kept safe and stays in reach. Partners get their scans back, and offline libraries that work without internet: 20,000 books for Science Gallery Bengaluru and 12,000+ for the RBANM Trust.

05Anyone with a phone

A Gandhi book, a Kannada novel or an old magazine, free to read online. The Servants Of Knowledge collection has 89,751 items and more than 18 million downloads.

06The people who scan

Every centre trains and employs local technicians, so the work builds skills and jobs along with the archive.

Origins

How SOK started

In 2015, American technologist Carl Malamud and Bengaluru-based engineer Omshivaprakash H.L. were each digitising Indian material on their own. They combined efforts and, almost by accident, made a private copy of the Government of India's digital library. When the government's own server crashed a couple of years later, that copy was the only thing that kept the collection alive online.

The organisation's formal work began in 2018 with a single scanner at the Indian Academy of Sciences in Bengaluru. When COVID-19 broke the supply chain for imported scanning equipment, the team did what Gandhian self-reliance would suggest: they reverse-engineered open-source scanner designs, with early support from the Internet Archive, and started building their own. Today SOK operates 15 self-built scanners, digitising 10–15 lakh pages every month.

In early 2024, the organisation was formally incorporated as a non-profit corporation under the name Anubhava Mantapa Servants of Knowledge Association, a Section 8 non-profit in India. The name is deliberate: Anubhava Mantapa was the assembly founded by the 12th-century social reformer Basava, often called India's first Parliament, built to transcend caste and gender and open access to knowledge for everyone. SOK shares that aim.

The motto

“Scanning is the New Spinning”

“Free data liberates us and stimulates our thought.” Carl Malamud, The Hindu, August 2024

During the freedom movement, Gandhi turned spinning khadi into a daily act of self-reliance and resistance: the charkha was both symbol and daily practice. Carl Malamud draws the same line for digital archiving: ordinary daily work that anyone can join, building something the whole country owns.

"Bread labour is the idea that you need to do something every day," Malamud told The Hindu in December 2024. "You have to put in the work every day. I think that's what Gandhi meant by bread labour." SOK calls its version of that daily discipline Gyan Satyagraha, a knowledge satyagraha fought with scanners, OCR software and open licences rather than confrontation.

2015

Two independent efforts join forces

Carl Malamud and Omshivaprakash H.L. combine their separate digitisation work, and mirror the Government of India's digital library, a copy that later proved critical.

2018

The first scanner

SOK begins digitising the collection of the Indian Academy of Sciences, Bengaluru, the organisation's first project.

2020–21

Building scanners in India

COVID-19 breaks import supply chains. The team reverse-engineers open-source scanner designs, with support from the Internet Archive, and starts manufacturing its own. There are now 15.

May 2023

Going public

SOK hosts a panel discussion on digital archiving and universal access at the Bengaluru International Centre, introducing the process to the public for the first time.

Oct 2023

Gandhi Bhavan partnership begins

Gandhi Bhavan, Bengaluru ties up with SOK to digitise its ~10,000-book library, the project that gave rise to "Scanning is the New Spinning."

2024

Incorporation, and 11,700 books handed over

SOK is formally incorporated as Anubhava Mantapa Servants of Knowledge Association. The Gandhi Bhavan library (11,700+ books, 129 AIR audio recordings, 12 languages) is digitised in three months and distributed to Gandhi Bhavans nationwide.

Feb 2025

33,149 books at NLSIU

A three-year effort to digitise the National Law School of India University's library is completed, alongside a searchable legal database for visually-challenged students built with IDIA.

Aug 2025

Offline libraries gifted

Ready-to-use digital library archives are presented to Bengaluru's Science Gallery (20,000 books) and the RBANM Trust (12,000+ books).

2026

Repub and Research Desk

Repub, SOK's open post-processing pipeline, and Research Desk, its open library platform, complete the chain from scanner to reader. OpenScribe, the system that captures the images and pushes them to Repub, is in progress.
