Our Story

Gyan Satyagraha — the quiet fight to open the doors of knowledge to all

Servants of Knowledge began as two people scanning books in their spare time. Today it's a registered non-profit running open-hardware digitisation centres across India — built on a simple conviction: knowledge that isn't free isn't really knowledge at all.

Origins

From two people with scanners to a national movement

In 2015, American technologist Carl Malamud and Bengaluru-based engineer Omshivaprakash H.L. were each digitising Indian material on their own. They combined efforts — and, almost by accident, made a private copy of the Government of India's digital library. When the government's own server crashed a couple of years later, that copy was the only thing that kept the collection alive online.

The organisation's formal work began in 2018 with a single scanner at the Indian Academy of Sciences in Bengaluru. When COVID-19 broke the supply chain for imported scanning equipment, the team did what Gandhian self-reliance would suggest: they reverse-engineered open-source scanner designs — with early support from the Internet Archive — and started building their own. Today SoK operates 18 self-built scanners, digitising 10–15 lakh pages every month.

In early 2024, the organisation was formally incorporated as a non-profit corporation under the name Anubhava Mantapa Servants of Knowledge Association — a Section 8 non-profit in India. The name is deliberate: Anubhava Mantapa was the assembly founded by the 12th-century social reformer Basava, often called India's first Parliament, built to transcend caste and gender and open access to knowledge for everyone. It is the same intention SoK carries into the 21st century.

The Motto

"Scanning is the New Spinning"

During the freedom movement, Gandhi turned spinning khadi into a daily act of self-reliance and resistance — charkha as both symbol and substance. Carl Malamud has drawn the same line to digital archiving: everyday, distributed, hands-on labour that anyone can take part in, building something the whole country owns.

"Bread labour is the idea that you need to do something every day," Malamud told The Hindu in December 2024. "You have to put in the work every day. I think that's what Gandhi meant by bread labour." SoK calls its version of that daily discipline Gyan Satyagraha — a knowledge satyagraha, fought not with confrontation but with scanners, OCR pipelines, and open licences.

“Free data liberates us and stimulates our thought.” Carl Malamud, Founder — The Hindu, August 2024

Timeline

Nine years, one lakh books

2015

Two independent efforts join forces

Carl Malamud and Omshivaprakash H.L. combine their separate digitisation work — and mirror the Government of India's digital library, a copy that would later prove critical.

2018

The first scanner

SoK begins digitising the collection of the Indian Academy of Sciences, Bengaluru — the organisation's founding project.

2020–21

Building scanners in India

COVID-19 breaks import supply chains. The team reverse-engineers open-source scanner designs, with support from the Internet Archive, and starts manufacturing its own — now 18 scanners strong.

May 2023

Going public

SoK hosts a panel discussion on digital archiving and universal access at the Bengaluru International Centre, introducing the process to the public for the first time.

Oct 2023

Gandhi Bhavan partnership begins

Gandhi Bhavan, Bengaluru ties up with SoK to digitise its ~10,000-book library — the project that gives rise to "Scanning is the New Spinning."

2024

Incorporation, and 11,700 books handed over

SoK is formally incorporated as Anubhava Mantapa Servants of Knowledge Association. The Gandhi Bhavan library — 11,700+ books, 129 AIR audio recordings, 12 languages — is digitised in three months and distributed to Gandhi Bhavans nationwide.

Feb 2025

33,149 books at NLSIU

A three-year effort to digitise the National Law School of India University's library is completed, alongside a searchable legal database for visually-challenged students built with IDIA.

Aug 2025

Offline libraries gifted

Ready-to-use digital library archives are presented to Bengaluru's Science Gallery (20,000 books) and the RBANM Trust (12,000+ books).

2026

Repub, launching at IndiaFOSS

SoK is set to publicly release Repub — open-source post-processing software that turns raw scans into searchable PDFs, hOCR and metadata — completing an end-to-end open pipeline.

The Team

Four people, four disciplines

SoK's core team spans open-data activism, engineering, legal research infrastructure, and copyright law — the exact combination a project like this needs.

CM

Carl Malamud

Founder & Honorary Secretary

US-based technologist and open-data advocate; founder of Public.Resource.Org. Called "The Internet's Own Instigator" by Wired. Three decades spent making government records, laws and standards public — from the SEC's EDGAR database to India's Bureau of Indian Standards.

OH

Omshivaprakash H.L.

Co-founder & Managing Director

Bengaluru-based engineer and digital archivist; co-founder of Sanchi Foundation. Leads scanner engineering, day-to-day operations and centre mobilisation across India, including the Mysuru digitisation centre.

SS

Dr. Sushant Sinha

Director / CTO

Computer scientist and founder of Indian Kanoon, India's leading free legal research platform. Leads OCR, search infrastructure and Indian-language processing for SoK's collections.

LL

Lawrence Liang

Director

Dean, School of Legal and Socio-Political Studies, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar University, Delhi. Guides SoK's approach to Indian copyright law so every collection is digitised and shared within it.
